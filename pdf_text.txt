Sentry AI -Powered Laser Tracking Turret  
 
1. Objective & Motivation :  
This project proposes an active, autonomous "Eye -to-Hand" tethered robotic sentry turret 
that actively tracks human subjects and dynamically targets specific body parts (head, 
chest, or hand) using a laser pointer. The goal is to build a real -time system t hat proves the 
viability of combining modern deep learning with classical kinematics, utilizing a high -
performance local compute node for complex vision processing and an embedded edge 
device for low -latency physical actuation.  
2. Hardware Architecture & Embedded Components  
To ensure a robust, low -latency tracking system, the physical architecture strictly divides 
computational heavy lifting from mechanical actuation. The hardware is structurally 
divided into processing, actuation, and power/data routing:  
• Central Compute Node (Vision & AI)  
• Local Processing Hub (Laptop/PC):  Serves as the primary vision sensor and 
AI processing engine. Utilizing an integrated HD Webcam and a dedicated 
GPU (e.g., RTX 4060), this hub captures the live feed, executes the deep 
learning pose -estimation pipeline, calculates the required inverse 
kinematics, and streams absolute physical angle commands over a high -
speed serial tether.  
• Processing & Vision Hub  
• Edge Microcontroller (ESP32 -CAM with OV2640 Sensor):  Repurposed 
strictly as a dedicated edge motor controller. It receives high -speed text 
commands via a physical serial tether and outputs stable Pulse Width 
Modulation (PWM) signals to drive the actuators, completely bypassing 
wireless network latency.  
• Serial Interface (ESP32 -CAM-MB Shield  or FTDI Programmer):  Utilized to 
establish the serial communication protocol and flash the initial C++ 
firmware to the microcontroller prior to wireless deployment.  
• Actuation & Targeting Payload  
• 2-DOF Actuators (2x SG90 Micro Servos):  Configured in a mechanical pan -
tilt linkage to execute the X -axis (yaw) and Y -axis (pitch) movements. These servos respond directly to the dynamic angle adjustments generated by the 
PID controller.  
• Targeting Indicator (5V Laser Diode):  Serves as the visual proof of targeting 
lock. It is mounted strictly parallel to the camera lens to eliminate parallax 
error and ensure spatial tracking accuracy.  
• Mechanical Chassis ( 1x SG90 Pan/Tilt Camera Bracket):  A structurally 
rigid, 3D-printed or standardized mounting bracket designed to house the 
actuators, camera, and laser payload as a singular moving unit.  
• Status Indicators (2x 5V LEDs):  Two colored LEDs (Red/Green) wired to the 
ESP32-CAM's GPIO pins to visually broadcast the system's current state 
(e.g., Idle/Scanning vs. Target Locked).  
• Power Distribution & Interconnects  
• Regulated Power Hub (5V DC Adapter & Breadboard):  A dedicated, 
hardwired 5V power source routed through a breadboard to ensure stable 
voltage delivery. This strictly separates the high -current draw of the servos 
from the logic -level signals of the ESP32 and TTL cable, preventing 
brownouts and signal degr adation.  
• Data Tether (USB -to-TTL Serial Cable):  Acts as the real -time nervous 
system of the project, providing a direct, zero -lag communication bridge 
between the Python AI pipeline on the compute node and the C++ firmware 
on the ESP32.  
• Logic Routing (DuPont Jumper Wires):  Standardized female -to-female 
interconnects for establishing a common ground and routing GPIO logic 
signals from the microcontroller to the servos.  
3. Technical Approach (Hybrid Methodology)  
To achieve real -time computational performance (>30 FPS) and accurate targeting, this 
project explores a hybrid architecture. The system is strictly divided into a Deep Learning 
Vision module (running locally on a  GPU) and a Classical Engineering control module 
executing the physical motor kinematics. This structure allows for a practical investigation 
into how modern AI and classical control theory interact.  
3.1 Deep Learning Pipeline (Vision & Tracking)  The AI pipeline handles detecting the human subject, extracting skeletal joints, and 
maintaining a persistent lock on the target across continuous frames.  
• Pose Estimation Benchmarking (Target Acquisition):  To select the optimal vision 
model, I will benchmark YOLOv8-Pose (optimized for GPU inference) against 
MediaPipe BlazePose  (optimized as a CPU baseline). This evaluation will be 
conducted in two phases to guarantee both mathematical accuracy and hardware 
viability:  
• Phase 1: Theoretical Accuracy (COCO -Pose): To objectively measure 
spatial accuracy without the massive overhead of manually labeling 
thousands of keypoints, the models will be evaluated against a subset of the 
industry-standard COCO-Pose dataset . This provides the baseline metrics 
for Precision, Recall, F1 -Score, and mAP , mathematically justifying the 
model's core intelligence.  
• Phase 2: Hardware -in-the-Loop Validation:  To mitigate "Domain Shift" 
(where models fail in real -world conditions despite high theoretical scores), 
the models will be tested directly against the live HD stream of the local 
tracking webcam . Because Ground Truth labels do not exist for a live stream, 
this phase will strictly evaluate Inference Latency (FPS)  on the local RTX 
4060 GPU, and monitor Confidence Score Degradation  caused by the 
camera's sensor noise and environmental lighting.  
• Multi-Object Tracking (Target Persistence & Ablation Study):  Because pose 
estimation models inherently suffer from frame -to-frame amnesia, the extracted 
keypoint coordinates must be processed through a Multi -Object Tracking (MOT) 
algorithm. To satisfy the project's experimentation requirement, I will conduct a 
comparative ablation study between ByteTrack  (a high-speed, purely mathematical 
tracker) and DeepSORT  (a tracker that integrates a secondary Convolutional Neural 
Network to analyze visual appearance).  
• Evaluation Dataset (Ground Truth Generation):  To mathematically test 
these trackers and fulfill the minimum 500 -sample dataset requirement, I 
will engineer a custom Evaluation Dataset. I will record a continuous video of 
myself moving with intentional environmental occlusions (e.g., walking 
behind fu rniture). By capturing this with the primary tracking webcam, I am 
actively testing the algorithms against the actual sensor hardware.  The video 
will be decomposed into sequential frames ($>500$ samples), and I will use 
semi-automated annotation software (like CVAT or Roboflow) to establish the 
"Ground Truth" bounding boxes.  • Tracking Metrics:  The tracking algorithms will be benchmarked against this 
Ground Truth Answer Key. Performance will be graded based on MOTA 
(Multi-Object Tracking Accuracy)  and the frequency of ID Switches  to 
quantitatively prove which algorithm exhibits superior target re -identification 
after an occlusion event.  
3.2 Classical Engineering Pipeline (Computer Vision & Kinematics)  
To create a true hybrid architecture, the system utilizes classical computer vision for 
efficient pre -processing and classical control theory for physical actuation. This prevents 
over-reliance on computationally expensive neural networks and ensures smoot h robotic 
movement.  
• Classical Computer Vision (The HOG+SVM "Wake -Up" Cascade):  Running heavy 
deep learning inference continuously on an empty room is computationally 
wasteful. To optimize system resources and ensure the system is environment -
agnostic (requiring no static background modeling), the pipeline utilizes a two -tier 
cascade : 
• Level 1 (Detection):  The raw video feed is scanned using a classical 
Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG)  paired with a Support Vector 
Machine (SVM)  classifier. This algorithm scans for the specific gradient 
vectors (outlines) of human pedestrians, ignoring environmental noise or 
non -human movement.  
• Level 2 (Activation):  Only when the HOG+SVM classifier confidently detects 
a human subject does the system "wake up" the YOLOv8 -Pose neural 
network to begin extracting specific skeletal keypoints. If the subject leaves 
the frame, the system returns to the low -power HOG scannin g state.

• Depth Estimation (Z -Axis Proximity):  Standard webcams do not provide 
native depth (Z -axis) information. To overcome this, the system calculates a 
pseudo-depth utilizing skeletal keypoint ratios rather than volatile bounding 
box dimensions. Because overall bounding box height fluctuates drast ically 
based on human posture (e.g., crouching versus standing), the algorithm 
instead extracts the pixel distance between the left and right shoulder 
keypoints generated by the YOLOv8 -Pose network. Since human bi -acromial

• Offset Calibration:  The algorithm must account for the physical disparity 
between the camera's lens and the turret's rotational center. A fixed physical 
translation vector (representing the X, Y, and Z distance between the laptop 
and the turret's base) is measured and loaded  into the kinematics algorithm 
to synchronize their coordinate spaces.  
• Dynamic Error Correction (Closed -Loop Calibration):  While the system 
primarily relies on predictive open -loop kinematics during live tracking, it 
mitigates physical hardware misalignment (e.g., webcam tilt, lens distortion, 
or non-planar mounting surfaces) via an automated pre -deployment 
calibration sequen ce. Prior to live operation, the turret is programmed to 
target three predefined spatial coordinates on a static background. The 
primary vision sensor detects the resulting laser projection within its 2D 
frame. By calculating the delta between the predicted kinematic location and 
the actual visual location, the system generates a dynamic error -correction 
matrix. This matrix is applied to the live inverse kinematics, effectively 
creating a pseudo -closed-loop system that offsets static physical 
inaccuracies and prevents drift at longer ranges.
